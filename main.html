What is Glossopedia?

Glossopedia is an online encyclopedia dedicated to constructed languages, unusual writing systems, ciphers, and linguistic artifacts. In other words, it is a digital resource that covers all linguistic phenomena that go beyond the scope of most people's everyday communication.

Accordingly, the name “Glossopedia” was chosen for the website, derived from the Ancient Greek words glossa (γλῶσσα), which translates as “tongue”, “language”, or “speech”, and paideia (παιδεία), meaning “upbringing”, “instruction”, and “education”.

The main sections to which the materials and articles posted on this website belong are described in detail below.

Constructed languages

A constructed language (conlang), also known as a planned language, is a human language whose sounds, grammar, and words are invented by a person or group, rather than growing naturally over time like natural languages (natlangs).

They fall into three main categories:

  • Auxiliary languages (auxlangs) are created to facilitate global or cross-cultural communication. These include: Interlingua, Interglossa, Glosa, and Esperanto.
  • Artistic languages (artlangs) are created to add realism and depth to the fictional worlds depicted in books and movies. Examples include Quenya (Tolkien), Klingon (Star Trek), and Dothraki (Game of Thrones).
  • Engineered languages (engelangs) are languages specifically created to test scientific, logical, or philosophical ideas. Some examples include: Toki Pona (minimalist), Lojban (logical), Láadan (gender-neutral), and Ithkuil (advanced morphology).

Unusual writing systems

Unusual writing systems are systems of writing, encoding, or concealing information that are distinguished by their specialized purposes, unusual structures, or historical and cultural significance.

Unlike widely used modern cryptographic algorithms, these systems are often associated with particular cultures, religious traditions, historical periods, or specialized intellectual practices. Some of them were designed to conceal information, while others served as alternative writing systems or methods of interpreting text.

Examples include:

  • Ogham is an early medieval writing system primarily used to represent the early Irish language. Its characters are formed from groups of strokes arranged along a central line.
  • Notarikon is a method of abbreviation and textual interpretation in which words or phrases are represented by their initial or other selected letters. It is particularly associated with Jewish textual traditions.
  • Temurah is a traditional method of transforming letters in a text according to established substitution rules. It is associated with Jewish interpretive and cryptographic traditions.

This section covers historical ciphers, alternative writing systems, letter-substitution methods, and other unusual approaches to encoding or representing language.

Linguistic and pseudolinguistic artifacts

Linguistic and pseudolinguistic artifacts are objects, texts, writing systems, or constructed forms of language that attract attention because of their unusual linguistic properties, uncertain origins, or relationship to known languages.

These artifacts may be genuine historical documents, experimental writing systems, fictional creations, or texts whose linguistic status remains disputed.

The section includes:

  • Undeciphered manuscripts — written documents whose scripts or languages have not been reliably deciphered. The Voynich Manuscript is a well-known example.
  • Artificial scripts — writing systems invented for fictional worlds, artistic projects, personal use, or experimental purposes.
  • Pseudolanguages — systems that resemble natural languages but lack sufficient evidence of being genuine naturally developed languages.
  • Fictional and experimental texts — works that imitate the appearance or structure of language without necessarily functioning as ordinary natural languages.

Glossopedia examines these artifacts from linguistic, historical, cultural, and structural perspectives, distinguishing documented facts from speculation and interpretation.

Goal and objectives

The goal of Glossopedia is to create an accessible online encyclopedia dedicated to unusual linguistic phenomena, constructed languages, and specialized writing systems.

The main objectives of the project are:

  • To collect and organize information about constructed and uncommon languages.
  • To describe unusual writing systems and ciphers.
  • To provide educational materials for students and linguistics enthusiasts.
  • To distinguish documented linguistic facts from speculation and pseudolinguistic claims.
